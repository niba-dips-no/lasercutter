# E-paper housing (3 mm board) — 2 housings

Outside 201 × 63.3 × 145.1 mm (width × depth × height), 217 mm wide with the ear tips. Window 165 × 100 mm for a 175 × 110 mm module.
Inside: 195 mm wide, 45.4 mm deep at the bottom, 18 mm at the top, 132 mm clear height for the screen.
Pins: cut four sticks about 13 mm long, 3 mm thick (skewers or dowel). Quantities below are for all 2 housings; 8 pins in total.

## Parts
- H1 Front ×2 — 217 × 137.5 mm
- H2 Back ×2 — 217 × 135.1 mm
- H3 Side ×2 — 63.3 × 145.1 mm
- H4 Side (USB) ×2 — 63.3 × 145.1 mm
- H5 Top ×2 — 200.4 × 17.5 mm
- H6 Bottom ×2 — 200.4 × 45.4 mm
- H7 Glass & screen frame ×2 — 194.4 × 130 mm

## Assembly
1. Glue the frame ring (H7) to the inside of the Front (H1), centred on the window.
2. Lay the cover glass (175 × 110 × 1.5 mm) in the ring's 177 × 112 mm window, against the Front. Stick it down with clear double-sided tape or a thin bead of silicone on the 5.0 mm border around the window only, never over the window. Then put the screen in behind it, a pad of foam behind that.
3. Lay the Side (USB) (H4) flat, outside face down, so the slots point up.
4. Stand Bottom (H6), Top (H5), Back (H2) and Front (H1) in their slots. The ears of Front and Back go all the way through.
5. Run the screen's flat cable back, and stick the ESP32 board to the inside of the Back with its USB-C port facing the slot in the Side (USB): about 9 mm in front of the Back and 25 mm up, so use roughly 5 mm of foam or spacers behind it. The board charges the battery itself. Put the battery on the Bottom.
6. Lower the other Side (H3) onto all the tabs and press it home.
7. Push a pin (3 mm stick) through the hole in each of the four ears. Glue is optional.
8. To open it: pull the pins on one side and slide that side off.

## Warnings
- Sizes of the FireBeetle ESP32-E board (assumed about 58 × 26 mm) and the RS PRO 1.8 Ah battery (assumed 50 × 34 × 8 mm) are guesses: measure both. A 1.8 Ah pack is often 8-10 mm thick.
